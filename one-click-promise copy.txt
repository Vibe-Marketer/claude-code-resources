# The One-Click Promise

**Every process should be reduced to as close to a single click as possible.**

This is the lens through which we see every product, every decision, every line of code.

---

## The Rule

**Simple scales. Complexity fails.**

We choose elegance over complexity—always. But when brilliant animations, flowing designs, or sophisticated automations *improve the customer experience*, bring it on. We don't avoid complexity. We avoid *unnecessary* complexity. If it serves the customer, we build it. If it's just fancy, we cut it.

Our code follows the same rule: bulletproof, do it right once. Not overcomplicated. Not clever for clever's sake. The best solution is the one that works, scales, and can be understood.

---

## The 10 Principles

1. **Gather Silently, Confirm Minimally** — Get the website. Scrape it. Present findings. "You sell X to Y—correct?" Yes/No. Store forever, reuse everywhere.

2. **Radical Simplicity** — Plain words. No jargon. 6th-8th grade level. "Who do you sell to?" not "Define your target avatar."

3. **Smart Defaults** — Every option has an intelligent default. Blank fields are failures.

4. **Invisible Complexity** — Users see simple. Systems handle complex.

5. **Respect Cognitive Budget** — Every decision costs the customer. Minimize withdrawals.

6. **Chain, Don't Fragment** — One intent, one action, complete. No shepherding through steps.

7. **One Click is the Ceiling** — Zero is better. If it can be anticipated and automated, do it.

8. **Fail Forward** — Errors resolve automatically or with one click.

9. **Challenge Everything** — "It's always been done this way" is not a reason. Nothing is impossible.

10. **Less is More** — One sentence beats a paragraph. Cut ruthlessly.

---

## Scope

- **Our customers** — Every product, feature, interaction
- **Their customers** — We enable one-click for end users too
- **Our team** — We operate this way internally
- **When we fail** — Fix it now. Then fix the system.

---

## The Decision Test

*Would a thoughtful person regret it if we just did this for them?*

- **No** → Do it. Don't ask.
- **Yes** → Ask once, simply, with a recommendation.

---

## For AI Agents

1. Check stored context — what do we already know?
2. Scrape before asking — analyze what exists
3. Confirm, don't interrogate — "You sell X?" not "What do you sell?"
4. Yes/No is the goal — transform questions into confirmations
5. Act when confident — inform what you did
6. Store what you learn — compound the knowledge base
7. If they're typing paragraphs, you failed

---

**One intention. One click. Done.**
